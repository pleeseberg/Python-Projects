PROJECT 2: Searching for C4H3N Isomers in Titan's Atmosphere
An Independent Analysis of ALMA Archival Data

Author: Paige Leeseberg
Status: Planning
Target Timeline: Months 2-4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
SCIENTIFIC QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Can propargyl cyanide (HCCCH2CN) and cyanoallene (H2CCCHCN) be detected
in Titan's atmosphere using publicly available ALMA archival data? If not,
what are the upper limits on their abundances, and how do these constrain
existing photochemical models?
 
WHY THIS MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project directly continues research begun at NASA Goddard Space Flight
Center. Methyl cyanoacetylene (CH3C3N) was confirmed on Titan in 2020
(Thelen et al.), making the other C4H3N structural isomers compelling
targets. These molecules have been detected in the interstellar medium,
and their presence or absence on Titan constrains the nitrogen and carbon
photochemistry that produces Titan's complex organic haze. This work picks
up where funding cuts left off.
 
TARGET MOLECULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Propargyl cyanide: HCCCH2CN
- Cyanoallene: H2CCCHCN
- Reference detected molecule: CH3C3N (Thelen et al. 2020, ApJL 903 L22)
 
DATA SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ALMA Science Archive (almascience.eso.org)
- Band 6: ~230-272 GHz
- Band 7: ~273-373 GHz
- Observations: 2014-2016 Titan datasets (now fully public)
- Access: Free account required, no cost to download
- Format: FITS data cubes, may require CASA calibration
 
METHODS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Data acquisition
   - Create ALMA archive account
   - Search for Titan observations in Band 6 and Band 7
   - Download calibrated data products where available
   - Install CASA for any recalibration needed
 
2. Spectral line identification
   - Pull molecular line frequencies from HITRAN and CDMS databases
   - Identify target frequency windows for each molecule
   - Extract spectra at Titan disk position
 
3. Signal search and upper limit calculation
   - Search for spectral line signatures at known frequencies
   - For non-detections: calculate 3-sigma upper limits
   - Apply beam dilution corrections
   - Account for Titan's limb vs disk geometry
 
4. Photochemical model comparison
   - Pull predicted abundances from Lavvas et al. and Vuitton et al. models
   - Compare upper limits to model predictions
   - Assess whether models are consistent with non-detections
 
5. Interpretation
   - Discuss chemistry implications
   - Identify which photochemical pathways are constrained
   - Suggest future observational follow-up
