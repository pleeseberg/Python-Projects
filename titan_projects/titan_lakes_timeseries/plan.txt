SCIENTIFIC QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How have the surface areas of Titan's northern hydrocarbon lakes changed
over the Cassini mission lifetime (2004-2017), and can we detect seasonal
variability consistent with Titan's ~29 year seasonal cycle?
 
WHY THIS MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Titan's methane/ethane lakes are unique in the solar system. Understanding
their seasonal behavior helps constrain Titan's methane cycle and informs
predictions for what NASA's Dragonfly mission (launching 2030s) will find
when it arrives. Any time series analysis of lake variability is directly
relevant to Dragonfly mission planning.
 
TARGET LAKES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Ligeia Mare (second largest, well observed)
- Kraken Mare (largest, polar)
- Punga Mare (smaller, good temporal coverage)
 
DATA SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- NASA Planetary Data System (pds.nasa.gov)
- Instrument: Cassini RADAR in SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) imaging mode
- Node: PDS Atmospheres Node (pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu)
- Time coverage: 2004-2017 (full Cassini mission)
- Access: Free, no account required
 
METHODS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Data acquisition and preprocessing
   - Download Cassini RADAR SAR images from PDS
   - Convert PDS format to workable arrays using astropy/pds4tools
   - Compile published lake surface area measurements from literature
 
2. Exploratory data analysis
   - Plot lake surface area vs time for each target
   - Identify gaps in temporal coverage
   - Look for obvious trends or seasonal signals
 
3. Time series analysis
   - STL decomposition (seasonal + trend + residual)
   - ARIMA modeling for trend characterization
   - Lomb-Scargle periodogram for unevenly spaced observations
 
4. Forecasting
   - Project lake behavior forward to Dragonfly arrival epoch
   - Quantify uncertainty on forecasts
   - Compare to published climate model predictions
 
5. Interpretation
   - Connect findings to Titan's known seasonal cycle
   - Discuss implications for methane replenishment models
   - Frame predictions for Dragonfly observations
