SCIENTIFIC QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How do uncertainties in photochemical reaction rate inputs propagate through
to predicted molecular abundances in Titan's atmosphere? Which reactions
contribute most to output uncertainty, and are current model predictions
actually consistent with observational upper limits given those uncertainties?
 
WHY THIS MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Titan's photochemical models (Lavvas, Vuitton, and others) are widely used
to interpret observations and guide molecular searches. However, the reaction
rates input to these models carry significant laboratory measurement
uncertainties that are rarely propagated through to the final abundance
predictions. This means we may be over- or under-confident in what molecules
should be detectable. A rigorous uncertainty quantification (UQ) analysis
using Monte Carlo methods is a genuine methodological gap in the literature
and a direct application of data science to planetary science.

This project also directly connects to Project 2 — the upper limits from
the C4H3N search can be used as observational constraints to validate the
uncertainty-propagated model outputs.
 
APPROACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rather than running a full photochemical model from scratch (which requires
atmospheric physics code), this project works with published model outputs
and their documented input reaction rates. We sample across the uncertainty
distributions of key reaction rates and propagate those samples through
simplified model relationships to generate posterior distributions on
predicted molecular abundances.
 
METHODS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Literature survey of reaction rate uncertainties
   - Extract key reaction rates from Lavvas et al. 2008 model
   - Document reported uncertainty ranges for each rate
   - Identify the ~20-30 most important reactions for C4H3N chemistry
 
2. Build Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation framework
   - Define prior distributions for each reaction rate input
   - Sample using Latin Hypercube Sampling (more efficient than random)
   - Propagate samples through model abundance relationships
   - Generate posterior distributions on predicted abundances
 
3. Sensitivity analysis
   - Use SALib for Sobol sensitivity indices
   - Identify which reaction rates drive the most output uncertainty
   - Rank reactions by their contribution to abundance uncertainty
 
4. Observational constraint comparison
   - Compare Monte Carlo output distributions to ALMA upper limits
   - Assess model-observation consistency under uncertainty
   - Identify molecules where observations meaningfully constrain models
 
5. Visualization and reporting
   - Corner plots of parameter posteriors (corner library)
   - Sensitivity ranking plots
   - Model vs observation comparison with uncertainty bands
